# Fork the City: product context

Fork the City helps community groups turn a real public place into a versioned, reviewable proposal. People branch from a documented baseline or public revision, edit spatial interventions, publish an immutable revision, compare alternatives, resolve merge conflicts, and track an approved proposal with evidence.

## Primary users

- Visitors inspect places, public revisions, comparisons, and public dossiers.
- Authenticated participants create private branches, publish revisions, comment, support, and report.
- Site coordinators promote a published revision into a dossier and record milestones and evidence.
- Site moderators handle reports for assigned sites with an audit trail.
- Operators load sites and catalogs and assign roles through documented procedures.

## Pilot and core story

The initial pilot is Plaza de la Constitución (Zócalo de Puebla), Mexico. Every proposed intervention is explicitly hypothetical unless a source proves it exists. The demo compares one proposal for shade with another for temporary market stalls, exposes their spatial conflict, and merges a resolved version without losing provenance.

## Product principles

1. Preserve provenance. A proposal never rewrites its source or a published historical revision.
2. Distinguish fact from scenario. Reference data, incomplete coverage, assumptions, and test fixtures must be visibly labeled.
3. Explain trade-offs. Show geometry, cost ranges, model inputs, coverage, and known limitations.
4. Keep decisions human. Support counts inform; they never trigger approval or state changes.
5. Work without a map. Every critical map action needs a textual or form-based alternative.
6. Protect privacy. Public aliases replace email addresses; no neighbor contact data is collected.
7. Use plain language in Spanish and English. Actions retain the same name from button to result.

## Initial success criteria

The first executable slice starts locally, shows the pilot place, continues to work when map tiles fail, and makes the difference between reference data and a hypothetical scenario unmistakable. The complete contest flow must be keyboard operable, usable at 360 px, and demonstrated with real test evidence rather than claims.
